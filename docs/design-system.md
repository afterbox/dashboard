# afterbox

**A gift for the people you love most.**

afterbox is a personal life vault you build over a lifetime for the people you love most: the accounts, policies and keys they'd need to find, the wishes they'd need to honour, and the messages you want them to hear. It stays sealed while you're alive, grows with you as your life does, and opens for them when the time comes.

afterbox also helps **any family that has just lost someone**, whether or not that person ever used afterbox, settle everything they left behind, one calm step at a time. It reaches many people through **employers, insurers and banks**, who offer it to their staff and customers.

Every decision in this system serves one feeling: **this was made with love.** Not security theatre, not legal admin, not grief. Warmth, care, and the quiet relief of someone who thought ahead.

## Brand essence

| | |
|---|---|
| **What we are** | A gift, built over years, received once. |
| **Who it's for** | The people you love most. The user builds it; *they* are who it's for. And families in loss, who need someone to help them through. |
| **How it should feel** | Like opening a letter from someone who loves you. Warm, unhurried, certain. |
| **What it is not** | A password manager, a will, a funeral service, a fintech dashboard. |

### Three ideas, always in this order
1. **Love first.** Lead with the people, never with death, money or features.
2. **It grows with you.** The vault is alive. A new home, a new child, a new message: it gets richer every year.
3. **It's safe, and it waits.** Sealed until it's needed. Then it opens gently, one step at a time.

## Three audiences, three modes

The same brand speaks to people in very different moments. Every screen belongs to one mode.

| Mode | Who | How it feels | Design rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gift** | People building their vault, and the landing page | Warm, hopeful, playful, a little joyful | Full palette. Rings, ribbon, `glow`, `ease-play`, the illustrated cast and signature motion are all allowed. Celebrate additions ("Added. One less thing for them to search for."). |
| **Still** | Families who have just lost someone: the executor dashboard, family support, the moment the vault opens | Quiet, steady, held | Mostly `linen`, `paper` and `ink`, with `keepsake` for weight. One `heartwood` action per screen. **No** `lantern`, no `glow`, no `ease-play`, no illustrated cast, no celebration, no confetti. Motion is only gentle fades (480ms or slower). One task at a time. |
| **Partner** | Employers, insurers and banks offering afterbox to their people | Trustworthy, clear, still warm | afterbox tokens stay in charge. The partner's logo sits in a lockup in the header only: *[Partner logo] · powered by afterbox*. A partner colour may replace `heartwood` only through `partner-accent`, and must pass the same contrast checks. Never change the type, the ground or the voice. |

### Still mode, in practice
- **Lead with care before tasks.** First screen: *We're so sorry. We'll take this one step at a time, together.* Then a single next step.
- **Never upsell in the first conversation.** A family in loss is not a lead.
- **Name what's done.** "3 of 12 done. Nothing else is urgent today." Progress reassures; a long list frightens.
- **Use the person's name.** "Your dad's accounts", "Margaret's pension", never "the deceased".

## Voice and content

**We sound like** a thoughtful friend who has been through it. Plain words, short sentences, warm but never syrupy. We say hard things honestly and kindly.

- **Write to the giver, about the receiver.** "Your daughter won't be guessing" beats "Organise your estate."
- **Name the people.** Use real-feeling names and relationships in examples: *your partner*, *your sister*, *Mary*. Never "beneficiaries" or "users" in product copy.
- **Honest about death, gentle about it.** Say "when you die" or "when the time comes" in context. Avoid euphemism piles ("pass on", "transition") and never lead a headline with death.
- **Calm, not urgent.** No fear-selling, countdowns or scarcity. The emotional pull is love, not anxiety.
- **Sentence case** everywhere, including buttons and headings.

### Lead with the gift, not the vault
In marketing (landing pages, ads, buttons, social), **never lead with "vault"**. It's a container, not a reason. Lead with the people and the gift: *for the people you love most*, *their gift*, *what they'll need*, *what you want them to hear*. Every line should carry both halves of afterbox: **love** (it's for them, from you) and **necessity** (they'll need it, they won't have to guess).
- "Vault" is fine inside the product and in the trust lines, where safety is the point ("Not even afterbox can open your vault").
- Buttons name the gift or the person, never the container: **Build their gift** (primary) · **Begin with someone you love** (the closing call to action, which mirrors the first step of onboarding: "Think of someone you love. Who came to mind first?").

### The name
Always **afterbox**: one word, all lowercase, even at the start of a sentence. Never "AfterBox", "After Box" or "TheAfterBox".

### Words we use / words we avoid

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| their gift, everything they'll need; "your vault" only inside the product | account, profile, database |
| the people you love most | beneficiaries, recipients, contacts |
| add, keep, leave | upload, input, submit |
| when the time comes | in the event of death, upon demise |
| sealed, opens, unlocks | decrypted, released, triggered |
| your executor (with a name) | the executor, administrator |
| message, letter, voice note | content, media, assets |
| the person who died, or their name | the deceased, decedent |
| settle, sort out, take care of | process, administer, close out |
| family support | bereavement services, claims handling |
| we'll introduce you to | we'll sell you, offers, deals |

### Examples
- Hero: *Build it for them. Before they need it.*
- Sub: *Everything they'll need to find, and everything you want them to hear. Gathered as your life grows, and given to them when the time comes.*
- CTA: *Build their gift* (primary) · *See how it works* (secondary) · closing: *Begin with someone you love*
- Empty state: *Nothing here yet. Start with the one thing they'd struggle to find.*
- Success: *Added. One less thing for them to search for.*
- Family support (still mode): *We're so sorry. Let's start with what matters this week.*
- Employer benefit: *A gift from [Company] to you and the people you love.*

### Trust lines (use these exact words)
Trust is the product, so these promises are always worded the same way:
- **Sealed:** *Not even afterbox can open your vault.* (We use split-key encryption: opening it needs a verified death plus your executor.)
- **Data:** *We never sell your data.*
- **No passwords:** *We never ask for passwords, PINs or card numbers.*
- **Advice:** *We guide you. We don't give legal or financial advice.*

### Introductions and fees
When we introduce someone to a partner (a will-writing law firm, a probate solicitor, an estate agent, an insurer):
- Always present it as an **option**, never a step they must take.
- Always disclose the fee, in plain words, next to the button: *We may receive a fee if you choose this firm. It doesn't change what you pay.* (Only use the second sentence if it's true.)
- Show it in `ink-muted` at `small` size, never hidden in a footnote.

## Colour

Warm paper, clay-rose, plum and gold. Think of a wooden keepsake box lined with linen and tied with a gold ribbon. Two themes: **Daylight** (default) and **Candlelight** (dark).

- **`linen`** is the ground everywhere. We never use pure white or pure black.
- **`heartwood`** is the brand and the action colour: one primary action per screen. It is named for the warm core of a tree, the oldest rings at the centre of something still growing.
- **`keepsake`** (plum) gives weight and intimacy: the footer, the "sealed" moment, large decorative blocks.
- **`lantern`** (gold) is the ribbon: decorative only. Never text on linen.
- **`sage`** means growth and done: an item added, an executor task completed.
- **`blush`** tints a single featured band or a personal message.
- **`alert`** is only for errors, always paired with an icon and words.
- **`on-heartwood`** and **`on-lantern`** are the only colours for text on those fills.

**Proportions on a page:** roughly 70% linen/paper, 15% ink, 8% heartwood, 7% keepsake/blush/lantern/sage combined.

**Never:** gradients from blue to purple, neon, cold greys, black-and-white "memorial" palettes, lilies-and-doves funeral imagery.

**Contrast:** every text pairing listed in the token notes meets 4.5:1 in both themes. `lantern` never carries text on linen.

## Typography

| Family | Face | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `display` | **Fraunces** (Google Fonts, variable, use `SOFT` 50–100) | Headlines. A warm, slightly soft old-style serif: human, crafted, literary. Set at weights 360–420, never bold. |
| `sans` | **Figtree** (Google Fonts) | Everything else: body, UI, buttons. Friendly, round, highly legible. |
| `note` | **Caveat** (Google Fonts) | The user's own handwriting voice. **Only** for the titles of personal messages or a quoted line from a letter. Never UI, never marketing claims. |

Rules:
- Headlines in `display`, sentence case, often one idea per line. Let them breathe (`space-12` below).
- On screens under 640px wide, use the `-mobile` display styles (see Mobile).
- Body copy max 64 characters per line.
- One `note` moment per screen at most; it should feel precious.
- Load: `https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Fraunces:opsz,wght,SOFT@9..144,300..600,0..100&family=Figtree:wght@400;500;600&family=Caveat:wght@500&display=swap` and set `font-variation-settings: "SOFT" 100` on display text.

## Layout and shape

- **Generous space.** Sections use `space-24` padding on desktop and `space-16` on mobile. Emotional moments get `space-32` of quiet around them.
- **Soft, boxy shapes.** Cards use `radius-lg`, large panels `radius-xl`, primary buttons `radius-pill`. Nothing is sharp-cornered.
- **Rest, don't float.** Cards sit on `rest`; only hover or an opened vault uses `lift`.
- **12-column grid**, 1200px max content width, 24px gutters; a single centred column of 640px for emotional storytelling sections.

---

## Mobile

afterbox is used as much on phones as on desktop, and families in loss will mostly use their phone. The same tokens apply; these rules adapt them.

- **Type:** below 640px wide, swap each display style for its `-mobile` version (`display-xl` → `display-xl-mobile`, 80px → 44px) and `body-l` for `body-l-mobile`. Body text never goes below 16px, and inputs stay at 16px so iOS doesn't zoom in.
- **Layout:** a single column with `space-5` (20px) side margins. Sections use `space-16` padding. Cards stack with `space-5` between them and keep `radius-lg`.
- **Thumbs first:** the one primary action sits at the bottom of the screen, full width, `radius-pill`, at least 52px tall, above the home indicator (respect safe-area insets). Touch targets are at least 44×44px.
- **Still mode on mobile:** one task per screen, a single large button, and a visible "done for today" exit. Never a long scrolling checklist.
- **Sheets, not modals:** confirmations and details open as bottom sheets on `paper`, with `radius-xl` top corners.
- **Browser and system bars:** set the browser `theme-color` to `linen` for each theme, `#fbf6ef` in Daylight and `#1c1416` in Candlelight, so the status bar blends into the page.
- **Motion:** keep signature moments short on mobile (900ms at most) and never block scrolling. The rings in the hero draw once, not in a loop.
- **Sunlight:** every text pairing passes WCAG AA, and core reading text (`ink` on `linen`) is above 15:1 in both themes, so it stays legible outdoors.

## Dark mode: Candlelight

Candlelight is a full theme, not an afterthought: every colour token has a Candlelight value, and every text pairing passes WCAG AA in it. The feeling is *a warm room lit by a candle at night*, not a black tech screen.

- **Follow the device.** Use the person's system setting (`prefers-color-scheme`) by default, with a manual switch in settings.
- **Never pure black.** The ground is a deep warm plum-brown (`linen` = `#1c1416`). That stays warm, avoids the harsh glare of white text on black, and avoids smearing on OLED phone screens when scrolling.
- **Elevation by light, not shadow.** Shadows barely show on dark screens, so raised things are a step lighter (`paper`) and every card has a 1px `line` border.
- **Colours shift, meaning doesn't.** `heartwood` becomes a softer coral and `keepsake` becomes a light plum used as a bright block. `lantern` stays gold, but text on it uses `on-lantern` (dark ink). Always use the tokens; never hard-code a light-mode hex.
- **Imagery:** photos get no filter, but avoid pure-white backgrounds in illustrations; illustrate on `linen` so they sit inside the theme.
- **Still mode suits Candlelight.** Many families will open afterbox late at night. Candlelight's low glare is part of the care.

## Motif: growing rings

Our signature shape is **concentric rings**, like the growth rings of a tree, radiating from one corner or centre. They stand for a vault that grows a ring with every year and every addition. Use them:
- behind the hero, slowly drawing outward as the page loads
- as section dividers (a quarter-ring in a corner)
- in the vault illustration, where each new item adds a ring

Draw rings as 1.5–2px strokes in `heartwood`, `lantern` or `linen` cut out of a block. Three to six rings, never a dense field.

Secondary motif: **the ribbon**, a single `lantern` line that ties sections together or wraps the vault box.

## Motion

Motion should feel like **unwrapping a gift and watching something grow**, never like a dashboard updating.

- **Easing:** `cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)` (`ease-gift`, a soft ease-out) for entrances; `cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)` (`ease-state`) for state changes; `cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1)` (`ease-play`, a small overshoot) for playful Gift-mode moments only: an item dropping into the vault, a character reacting, a nudge card popping in. Never `ease-play` in Still mode.
- **Durations:** 200ms for hover and press, 480ms for reveals, 900–1400ms for signature moments (rings drawing, the vault lid opening).
- **Signature moments:**
  1. *Rings grow* outward from the hero as it loads.
  2. *Items drop in* to the vault one by one as you scroll, each adding a ring and a brief `glow`.
  3. *The lid lifts*: the sealed vault opens and messages rise out, addressed to named people.
- **Scroll reveals:** fade up 16px, staggered 80ms. Never parallax that fights reading.
- **Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`:** replace movement with simple fades and draw rings instantly.
- **Still mode:** no signature moments except *the lid lifts*, and that one is slower (1400ms+) and quieter: no `glow`, no rings drawing. Everything else is a fade.

## Imagery and iconography

- **Photography:** warm, natural light, real homes and hands: a parent and child at a table, an old letter, a kitchen in the evening. Never stock grief (gravestones, black clothing, rain on windows) and never staged corporate smiles.
- **Illustration:** flat shapes in the brand palette, built from the rings, ribbon and rounded boxes. No 3D, no brand mascot. Gift mode can use the illustrated cast (see "Illustration and the cast").
- **Icons:** none chosen yet. Use a rounded line set at 1.5px stroke on a 24px grid, in `ink` or `ink-muted`; `heartwood` only for the active state.
- **Logo:** none yet. Until a mark exists, set the wordmark **afterbox** in Fraunces at weight 420, `SOFT` 100, in `ink` (or `linen` on `keepsake`).

## Illustration and the cast

afterbox is playful where life is playful (inspired by Duolingo's warmth and Lemonade's everyday characters) and tender where it's tender. An original illustrated cast carries the playfulness, **in Gift mode only**.

- **Who:** a family (you, your partner, a daughter who grows up across a story, a grandparent), **Biscuit the family dog**, and everyday things: a house, a bird on the roof, clouds, a kitchen table, letters, the box.
- **Biscuit** is the one recurring character. He's part of the family, not a brand mascot: he never talks, never sells and never appears in the logo. He guides the eye between scenes, reacts when something is added (sits up, wags), and ages gently across a story (puppy → grey muzzle). He's a nod to "what happens to the dog?", one of the wishes people record.
- **Style:** flat, rounded shapes cut from the radius scale; outlines at 2px in `ink` (Daylight) or `linen` cut-outs on blocks; fills only from the palette (`heartwood`, `keepsake`, `lantern`, `sage`, `blush`, `paper`). Faces are simple (dot eyes, a small curve for a smile), friendly and inclusive, with varied skin tones from a warm, natural range. No gradients, no 3D, no photoreal detail.
- **Movement:** characters can bounce, wag and react with `ease-play`. They never mock, joke about death or appear in distress.
- **Still mode:** no cast antics. At most, a still, quiet presence (an old dog resting by the door). Bereavement screens and the family-support flow use no characters at all, only objects (the box, letters, a lamp).
- **Composition:** draw key scenes twice, landscape for desktop and portrait for mobile, rather than shrinking one.

## Scroll storytelling (landing pages)

The landing page is a story you scroll, inspired by Mercury's zoom and Framer's section-to-section flow.

- **One section, one idea, one feeling.** Each section has its own motion signature so no two feel the same (zoom, typing, drop-and-bounce, slow paper, mechanical clasp, timeline rhythm, slow lift).
- **Hand-offs:** every section ends by setting up the next (papers gather into the box; envelopes slide in and the lid closes), so people want to keep scrolling.
- **The zoom:** the hero zooms *in* through the growth rings to the box; the final scene zooms back *out* to the house at night. Same scene, bookends.
- **Pinned scenes:** about 250vh on desktop, about 180vh on mobile. They never trap the scroll.
- **Performance:** animate only `transform` and `opacity`; lazy-load later scenes; the hero is interactive within 2.5s on a mid-range phone.
- **Reduced motion:** every scene has a designed final frame that is shown still.

The full landing page story lives in the afterbox codebase at `docs/landing-page-story.md`.

## Accessibility

- All text pairings meet WCAG AA in both themes (see token notes).
- Focus: 2px `focus` ring, 2px offset, on every interactive element.
- Never use colour alone for meaning: `alert` and `sage` states always carry an icon and words.
- Touch targets at least 44×44px.
